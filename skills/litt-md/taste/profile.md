# litt.md taste profile

> v1.10 · built from 2 interviews, 3 audits (8 sources), 1 type trial (14 faces), 1 components trial (8 components), 1 side-by-side trial (7 components studies), 1 motion proposal (14 decisions), 1 open-decisions review (26 decisions), 1 tag trial (4 options), 1 reference set (5 shots), 2 split tests (6 type versions, 4 neutrals), 2 controls canvases (Nick's own frames, then the rebuild, the rules and every other control), 1 Flat system page (45 components at five shapes), 165 journal entries · November 2026

This is Nick's design language, written as nine rules you can check. Every rule has an ID (`R#`) so journal entries can point at it, and a **Check** line so a critique can say pass or fail rather than "feels off". Version 1.0 replaced the twelve rules of v0.x and their Do and Don't lists; the Changelog maps the old IDs to the new.

## Point of view

**Precise instruments, lit from above.** The screen is exact and quiet: an 8px grid, hairlines, one family in three weights by role, on warm stone neutrals. What a hand touches is an object: a block that sits in a tray, a cap that rides in a slot, glass that floats over the page, all lit by one light from above. Color barely appears; where it does, it is the one accent acting as light, or it carries data. Every state is said in words.

Five words: **precise, lit, airy, calm, exact.**

## The nine rules

**R1. Precise instruments.** Screens are exact and quiet; the controls are objects you can hold. Nothing is decorated: anything on screen is an object a hand uses, the one light, content, or data. Every state of every object is designed with the same care: default, hover, pressed, focus and disabled, and empty, loading or error where they apply.
- *Check:* remove the press material and the page still reads as finished. A gradient, a texture or a shape that is none of those fails (a purple-to-blue hero, gradient text, a gradient border), and so does a component with an undesigned state.

**R2. Light from the top.** One light, from above, on everything. Raised things catch it on their top edge (`--highlight`), cut things (a slot, a tray) on their lower wall, and floating things glow evenly, with no offset. Each theme has its own light and material values (grain 2% in light and 4% in dark, the light tint 12% and 22%, glass and borders tuned apart); neither theme is the other flipped. Material is still unless a hand is on it: glow, grain and gradients never animate, pulse or follow the cursor, and under a press a shadow changes at once and comes back on release. Flat has no light: no highlight, no lit wall, no cast; floating things keep their glow and ring.
- *Check:* light that disagrees with a source above fails: a highlight on the top edge of something cut or the lower edge of something raised, or glows from two directions. Any animation or transition on background-image, filter or glow opacity fails (soft focus under R4 is the one filter that moves). Both themes are specified for every surface and material token in `tokens.css`; the check script verifies it.

**R3. The depth ladder.** Four depths, one recipe each, and nothing else.
- **Sunken:** a tray (`--tray`, `--tray-shade`: a hairline and the top wall's shade), a cut (`--cut` inside the control line, with `--cut-shade`, the top wall's shade), or a slot cut into a plate (`--slot`, `--slot-shade`, which adds the lit lower wall). Where a value lives or a hand goes in: a tray holds choices, a cut is what you type or put a mark into (a field, a checkbox's box, a radio's ring, a color well), and a slot is what fills (a slider, a scrubber, progress).
- **Flat:** the canvas and surfaces 1 to 3, with a hairline and the top highlight, never a shadow. Where content sits: cards, panels, rows.
- **Raised:** a block (`--block`, `--block-lift`, and the tighter `--block-press` while pressed), or a cap and the plate it rides on, which stand taller (`--cap-lift`, `--cap-press`, `--plate-lift`). What a hand presses or moves: a button, a select's trigger, a stepper's − and +, a key cap, a tag you can lift out of a field. Only raised things cast, straight down, never more than 3px. Selected is raised: a switch's block, the selected segment, the current page in a nav and a picked card are the same block. A mark (a tick, a radio's ink and its bead) stands flush in its cut.
- **Floating:** glass, only on what floats over content (the sticky header, menus, the command menu, popovers, dialogs and toasts), with a zero-offset glow and a 1px ring (`--shadow-tooltip`, `--shadow-float`, `--shadow-modal`). It earns its blur over busy content: never on a card in the flow, never glass on glass.

Every depth survives forced colors: a block and its tray keep a 1px border, and the selected item takes the system highlight.

**Under Flat** (`[data-material="flat"]`) the same four depths are said by fill alone, on neutral greys, in Flat's own language (v1.9, `proposals/flat-pill.md`), five rules: one shape for every control, a pill by default (Flat sets the Pill shape, controls 28px and surfaces 16px, and every other Customize shape still works, since every corner reads the radius tokens; a key with one symbol is a circle); an outline means off (the only line left is the 3:1 `--line-control` edge of a control that is off: a switch, an unticked checkbox, an unpicked radio, a step to come); ink means on (a switch that is on, a ticked box, a committed value, in `--fg` with `--canvas` on it, never the accent, so R6's one accent fill holds; the accent stays for the one primary action and the one main switch); a grey bubble means chosen (the current tab, page, segment or row, and every field, is a `--surface-3` fill with no outline; focus is the ink ring); and no lines between things (rows, tables, menus and groups part with space and type; no dividers, no frames). Sunken: a tray has no fill and no frame, a cut is `--surface-3` with no line and no shade, and a slot is `--surface-3`, its fill a step darker. Flat: the canvas and surfaces with no highlight. Raised: a block is a `--surface-3` fill and a cap a mid grey, and neither casts nor sinks (`--press-depth` is 0). Floating keeps its float shadow. Under forced colors a grey bubble keeps a system outline, so chosen still shows. Press and Device keep their recipes unchanged.

**Under Device** (`[data-material="device"]`) the same four depths are said in hardware, after the components studies (`proposals/device.md`). Sunken: a tray is a well cut into the body; a cut you type into is LCD glass (`--field`, `--field-ink`, `--field-shade`), with its text in the mono, while a cut you mark (a checkbox, a radio) is a small light well; a slot is a well with a lit fill. Flat: the canvas is an aluminium body and surfaces are plates on it, lit on their top edge. Raised: a block is a key on a 2px skirt, a cap a knob of the key's face. Floating: glass, as under Press. The accents, status inks and data hues are retuned to the body (`--device-*`), and every pair is checked again under Device.
- *Check:* a shadow or inset that is not one of these recipes fails; the check script bounds them (no sideways offset, a raised cast at most 3px down, an inset shade blurring at most 2px). A field, a checkbox or a radio that is not a cut fails, and so does a select drawn as a field. A shadow on a card in the flow fails, glass on something that does not float fails, and a current or selected item marked only by text color, a dot, an underline or the accent fails. In forced colors a block and its tray stay two shapes. Under Flat, a shadow other than a ring or a floating thing's glow fails.

**R4. Motion answers a hand.** Each material has its own motion language (v1.8): under Flat things come from where you touched them (a set deals out of what opened it and gathers back, an overlay grows out of its opener from 0.9); under Press things have weight (a set settles from half of `--travel` above, an overlay rises and settles from `--press-scale`, nothing flies); under Device things are mechanisms (a set and an overlay switch on in place in three steps on the instant tier, with no growth or slide). Three tiers answer a click: instant (a 100ms tween) for hover, press and color; responsive (spring, 200ms, bounce 0) for what moves in place; expressive (spring, 350ms, bounce 0.12), once per view, for a dialog, a toast or a list entrance. Exits run at 0.75× and never bounce. Under a hand: a dragged part follows 1:1 with no easing and holds where it was taken; on release it settles on the responsive spring; a value it can come home to (the default) holds it a little; a press sinks it half a pixel; pushed fast, a cap may lean into the move, at most 4°, and rights itself on the spring. Menus and dense lists move one highlight from row to row. Only transform and opacity move, plus color, background-color, border-color, fill and the focus ring fading at instant. Nothing moves on its own, and reduced motion stills it all. A content swap a hand causes (a carousel step, a caption, a tab's panel) may arrive in soft focus: the fade with a blur of at most 4px, on the responsive spring; never material, never on scroll. Under Device a key sinks its whole 2px skirt when pressed (`--press-depth`), the one press deeper than half a pixel; reduced motion keeps it still.
- *Check:* a spring bouncing past 0.12, a bounce on the responsive tier or on an exit, an instant tween over 240ms, or a view with two expressive moments fails. Animating width, height, top or left, or `transition: all`, fails. A dragged part that jumps under the pointer fails. Motion that is not a response to the user (scroll-triggered reveals, looping autoplay, parallax, a marquee, a pulsing or shimmering skeleton) fails. A blur that moves fails, except soft focus: at most 4px, on a swap a hand caused.

**R5. Say it in words.** A state, a status and a value are said in words: a switch says On or Off, a slider its value (1.0×), a deploy Ready or Failed. Never by color, a dot or position alone. A word inside a control is sentence case, at 12px and the title weight.
- *Check:* hide color and motion, and every state still reads. A status or a current page marked with a dot fails, and so does a toggle whose state is only its position or its color.

**R6. One accent, as light.** The accent appears as a flat fill once per view: on the primary action, with its glow (`--glow`), or on the one switch that is the view's main choice, which never glows. Elsewhere it is light: the focus ring (2px `--focus` at a 2px offset, on everything focusable) and links (`--accent-text`). Checkboxes, radios and every other switch fill with `--fg`, because a form can hold many. Litt's accent is Blue 4: its hover is a step darker in light and lighter in dark, and its text tone lifts in dark until it passes 4.5:1 on the page and on its own tint. The accent stands apart from the data hues: its fill at least 0.12 from every data hue's mark, and its text at least 0.12 from every data hue's text, in OKLab, in both themes. A project may run monochrome instead (`[data-accent="mono"]`): the primary action takes the ink, with a block's lift for a glow. Or it may run pink (`[data-accent="pink"]`), held to every accent rule above: white text on its fill, its text tone at 4.5:1 on the page and its tint, and 0.12 from every data hue, rose included, which is why its text is a deep berry in light and a pale pink in dark.
- *Check:* count the accent fills in the view; more than one fails, and a checked checkbox, radio or second switch in the accent counts as one. A background filled with the accent fails, and so does an accent switch with a glow. The check script measures the distance to the data hues. A data hue (R7) is not the accent and does not count.

**R7. Color carries data.** Beyond the one accent, color appears only where it says something: which series or category a mark belongs to, how much (a step of a heat scale), which side of a goal a value falls on, or who or what a thing is (a category tag, an avatar). It comes from the five data hues, `--data-blue`, `--data-amber`, `--data-teal`, `--data-violet` and `--data-rose`, assigned in that order and never cycled. Each has a mark tone (3:1 on every surface), a `-text` tone (4.5:1, also on its own tint) and a `-tint` fill. Chrome never takes a data hue: buttons, controls, surfaces, borders, nav, rails, gridlines and running text stay neutral, and the accent keeps the primary action. Within one chart or one list a hue means one thing. Status stays with success, warning and danger, and a word (R5). A destructive action is a block with words in `--danger`; the one button filled with danger is the confirm of the dialog that asks, and it lies flat until the dialog's condition is met. A status mark (a failed bar, a danger tick on a timeline) is data, not a fill. A single quantity may stay in the foreground, which is the quiet default.
- *Check:* a data hue on a button, a control's fill, a surface, a border, a nav row, a rail or a gradient fails. One hue for two meanings in a chart or a list fails, and so does a hue picked only to brighten. A color outside the five fails, and a sixth series folds into "Other" or a second chart. A button filled with danger anywhere but a destructive dialog's confirm fails. A hue's mark tone or tint used as text fails (the lint checks it): text in a hue takes its `-text` tone.

**R8. Weight, ink and space rank.** Three weights, one per role, and nothing else. Text is 400 (`--weight`): reading, body, small, nav and descriptions. Titles are 500 (`--weight-title`): display, h1 to h4, the title in a title-and-description lockup, buttons, tab and segment labels, words in a control, `b` and `strong`. Labels are 600 (`--weight-label`), at 12px only: the caption step, tags, meta rows and table headers. Two inks: `--fg` for titles and key values, the grey for everything else (`--fg-muted` for body and `--fg-subtle` for metadata carry the same grey; keep both names, so the two can split again with one edit). Nearly everything is 14px with one 18px page title, and below it rank comes from weight and ink, never size. Lockups are tight and gaps wide: a label 4px above its title, a title 4px above its text, blocks 16px apart, sections 48px. Every figure is tabular, and the mono is for code only. Icons are ink too: one solid set on a 16 grid, a filled shape with its details cut out, and a mark that is only a line (a chevron, an arrow, plus, a check) a 1.5 stroke, so an icon weighs what a 14px word weighs. Under Device, what you type sits behind glass in the mono at 400 (`--field-font`), and a 12px label is an engraved capital (`.litt-legend`: uppercase, tracked, a one-pixel engraving); outside Device the same tokens leave every label as it is.
- *Check:* any computed font-weight other than 400, 500 or 600 fails, including browser defaults (`tokens.css` sets `h1`–`h6`, `b` and `strong` to the title weight, `th` to the label weight, and turns off faux bold). 500 or 600 on running text fails, 600 above the caption size fails, and the mono above 400 fails. `font-medium`, `font-semibold` or `font-bold` in markup fails: `font-title` and `font-label` are the role utilities. Type above 18px fails, and so does a heading stepped down by size below the page title. A second typeface fails: Google Fonts loads Geist at 400, 500 and 600 only, and Geist Mono at 400 only. The mono outside code and terminal output fails. An outline icon, an icon from another set, or an icon heavier than the text beside it fails. The check script scans the assets, and the gallery lint the components.

**R9. Exact, with room.** Spacing sits on the 8px grid (4px inside components), and borders are 1px hairlines; light and shadow may use half pixels, edges and spacing may not. Radii are 4px for controls, 8px for surfaces and the pill, nested so corners stay concentric: a surface is 8px, and a control 4px inside it sits 4px from its edge. Controls share one height scale, sm 32, md 44 and lg 56 (`--control-*`), with labels trimmed to their cap height (`.litt-trim`), so at sm and md the side padding roughly equals the air above and below it: sm pad 12, md pad 16. An lg button runs wider, pad 32 (`--button-pad-lg`), and an lg field keeps pad 24. lg is for hero CTAs only: a page form's and a dialog's fields and actions are md, and a field is lg only beside an lg button. On a touch screen every target is at least 44px. Pages are airy: one idea per section, 128px between sections on desktop, 96 on tablet and 64 on mobile (`--section-y`), composed asymmetric and left-aligned, centered only for one short statement. Tools may step down to sm and tighten their rows, never off the scale.
- *Check:* spacing off the scale (20, 28 or 36px; `p-5`, `p-7`) fails, a 2px or heavier decorative border fails, and a radius off the scale fails. Control heights come only from the scale; for every sm and md button, side padding ÷ vertical air (from the trimmed label to the edge) sits between 0.85 and 1.25, and for an lg button between 1.3 and 1.6. A hero CTA below lg fails, and so does an lg button in a page form or an lg field or button in a dialog. A touch target under 44px fails. More than one headline-level idea in a section fails, and so does a page where every section is centered.

## Generic-AI tells this language must avoid

Claude's default output drifts toward these. Each one is banned unless the session explicitly asks for it.

- Purple-to-blue gradient hero on white or black.
- Emoji as section markers or bullet icons.
- Everything centered, section after section.
- An accent bar or rail on the left edge of rounded cards.
- An arrow suffix (`→`) on every link and button.
- One radius (`rounded-lg`, 12px, 16px) applied to every element. Litt has two on purpose: 4px controls and 8px surfaces, plus the pill.
- The same uppercase mono eyebrow above every section ("01 / FEATURES").
- Three equal feature cards with an icon, a title and two lines, as the default answer to every section.
- One word of the headline picked out in accent color.
- Inter or Space Grotesk, or a default system stack, as the "safe" face. litt.md sets Geist, as on litt.design: 14px, three weights by role, two inks and tight lockups. Any sans at 16px and 400 everywhere, with headings scaled up by size, is still the tell.

## Open questions

The interview workflow works through these. When one is answered, move the answer into a rule (`R#`) or decision and delete it here.

- **Q5.** Art direction for imagery: product screenshots only, 3D renders, photography, abstract light studies?
  - *Evidence:* product UI floating over atmospheric, blurred photography or painting shows up on the Cosmos board (about 8 pins) and on Cursor's hero.
  - *Suggested (v1.0 review):* product UI first; a light study as the one atmospheric image.
- **Q6.** Does the signature apply to client work, or only to Litt's own properties?
  - *Suggested (v1.0 review):* the material does everywhere, since it is the controls; the atmospheric light does not.
- **Q7.** Texture: noise grain or a dot grid / dot matrix?
  - *Evidence:* the v0.11 reference set (five shots of dark data panels) uses dot matrices as data (a usage grid, a reply-time distribution) and a dot grid behind a 2D control. Litt now draws dots as marks and as a coordinate grid inside a control, not as page texture.
  - *Evidence:* litt.design has a dot-grid background, the Pinterest "OS" board is about half dot-matrix and pixel patterns, and dither appears on Cosmos.
  - *Suggested (v1.0 review):* neither as page texture; dots stay data marks and coordinate grids.
- **Q11.** Exposed editorial structure (visible column rules, index numbers) shows up a lot on the Pinterest "web" board. Allow it as a secondary device on content pages, or keep the language purely light-and-material?
  - *Suggested (v1.0 review):* column rules as hairlines on long reads only; no index numbers.
- **Q12.** Which theme comes first on marketing pages when the system preference is unknown? Cursor, Vercel and litt.design are all light-first.
- **Q16.** Litt's own default accent: the gallery opens in monochrome (2026-10-27, "make the monochrome default"), while the registry still ships Blue 4 as `:root`, with monochrome and pink behind `data-accent`. Should the language follow the gallery, so an install is monochrome until a project picks a color?
  - *Evidence:* Nick asked for monochrome as the default in the same message that added pink; the request was about the gallery's Customize panel, so only the gallery changed.
  - *Suggested:* follow the gallery. Monochrome is the quietest reading of R1 and R6 ("color barely appears"), and a color becomes a choice a project makes. It changes what every install looks like, so it is a `breaking` `litt` entry with a `Migrate:` line (`data-accent="blue"` to keep Blue 4).
- **Q14.** Mono: keep Geist Mono next to Geist, or switch to JetBrains Mono, Fragment Mono or IBM Plex Mono?
  - *Evidence:* Nick is "tired of Geist" but only replaced the sans. The 14-face type trial (v0.4) paired its sans with JetBrains Mono by default.
  - *Evidence:* v0.19 narrows it to code and terminal output only ("Lets use Mono only for code/terminal"). The face question still stands, for code alone.
  - *Suggested (v1.0 review):* keep Geist Mono; it only sets code now, so the stakes are low.
- **Q17.** The registry's default material. The gallery opens in Flat; installs get Press until a project sets `data-material="flat"`.
  - *Evidence:* Nick chose Flat as a choice beside Press, with the gallery opening in it (2026-11-01), the way monochrome became the gallery's accent before the registry's (Q16).
  - *Suggested:* decide after the components theme, when Flat has its own recipes and can be judged whole.
